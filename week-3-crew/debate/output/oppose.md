**Argument Against the Motion: Strict Laws to Regulate LLMs**

Ladies and gentlemen, while the risks of LLMs are real, imposing strict laws now is a dangerous overreaction that would cripple innovation and do more harm than good. First, strict regulation assumes we fully understand a technology that is still in its infancy. Lawmakers cannot keep pace with rapid developments; rigid rules would lock in outdated standards, discouraging research and driving talent overseas. Second, many of the feared harms—misinformation, bias, fraud—are already covered by existing laws on defamation, fraud, and discrimination. Adding duplicative, LLM-specific red tape creates legal chaos and burdens startups while leaving loopholes for bad actors overseas. Third, strict laws would mandate transparency that is often impossible without exposing trade secrets or enabling adversarial attacks, paradoxically reducing security. The better path is flexible, adaptive frameworks like voluntary safety standards, industry self-regulation, and incremental case law. Over-regulating prematurely would hand the future of AI to authoritarian regimes who care nothing for safety. Innovation and responsibility are not mutually exclusive—but rigid prohibition often breeds both stagnation and black markets. Let’s not shackle a tool that holds immense promise for education, healthcare, and creativity. Instead, let’s nurture its growth with guidance, not chains. Oppose strict laws.
